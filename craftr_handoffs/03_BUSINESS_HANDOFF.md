# Craftr — Business Handoff

## Category
Craftr should not be positioned as:
- 3D printing
- CAD
- hardware consultancy

Position it as:
**AI-native physical product creation infrastructure.**

## Initial customer
Start with people who understand software/product but not hardware:
- indie developers
- startup founders
- product designers
- makers
- agencies
- early R&D teams

Do not start with "everyone."

## Business model
Craftr can eventually monetize across four layers.

### 1. SaaS
Potential pricing:
- Free: limited experiments
- Pro: roughly ₹2k–₹5k/month or equivalent global pricing
- Teams / enterprise: higher ACV

### 2. Usage
Charge for:
- generations
- simulations
- complex builds
- export / validation credits

### 3. Manufacturing margin
User orders physical prototype through Craftr.

Example:
- electronics: ₹1,500
- printing: ₹500
- assembly/testing: ₹500
- shipping: ₹200
- cost: ~₹2,700
- sell: ~₹4,500–₹6,000

### 4. Marketplace take rate
Creators publish hardware products.
Other users remix and order them.
Craftr takes ~10–20%.

## Scalability condition
Craftr is scalable only if human engineering effort per product falls over time.

Track:
**Human minutes per successful manufactured design.**

If every order needs hours of engineer involvement, Craftr becomes a consultancy.

## Why this can scale
Many products can be generated from a relatively small set of reusable primitives:
- common compute modules
- common sensors
- batteries
- displays
- standard enclosure patterns
- standard power architectures

The user perceives infinite variation while the system reuses verified modules underneath.

## Market thesis
Adjacent categories are large:
- CAD / PLM
- EDA
- 3D printing
- prototyping
- contract manufacturing
- embedded tooling

But Craftr's larger opportunity is not simply taking share from CAD.

The category expansion thesis is:
> Millions of people do not build hardware today because the skill barrier is too high.

Craftr expands the number of people who can create physical products.

## Long-term business evolution
### Stage 1
AI hardware prototyping

### Stage 2
Lovable for hardware

### Stage 3
Hardware marketplace

### Stage 4
Physical-product infrastructure / API

Long-term API vision:

ProductSpec → Craftr → manufacturable physical product

## Manufacturing strategy
Do not own factories early.

Own:
- intent layer
- design intelligence
- Blocks
- validation
- manufacturing orchestration

Partner for:
- 3D printing
- PCB fabrication
- CNC
- injection molding
- assembly
- logistics

## Potential moat
- verified hardware Block library
- compatibility graph
- failure data
- real-world manufacturing feedback
- supplier / quoting data
- product designs
- marketplace network effects

## Long-term vision
Craftr could become something like:
**Shopify + AWS + App Store for physical-product businesses.**

A founder should eventually be able to start a hardware company without first building a hardware engineering team.
